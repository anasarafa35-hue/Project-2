3) Hanging Gardens of Babylon (Iraq)

The Hanging Gardens were filled with plants and trees. They were built in a dry land. Water flowed through the gardens. This made them rare.

The gardens were built on high terraces. Water was lifted from a river. This required smart engineering. Technology was advanced.

Many believe the gardens were built for a queen. She missed her green homeland. The gardens showed love. They also showed power.

No clear ruins have been found. Historians still debate them. Ancient writers described them. Their story lives on.

Fun Facts

  • The gardens were said to look like a green mountain.
  • They may have been near the Euphrates River.
  • Exotic plants were brought from far away.

How It Was Built..

Construction

The Hanging Gardens were believed to be built around 600 BCE during the reign of King Nebuchadnezzar II. The gardens were described as a series of terraces rising upward, supported by stone columns and platforms. These terraces were filled with soil and planted with trees, flowers, and vines. An advanced irrigation system was used to lift water from the Euphrates River to the top levels of the garden. This may have used a chain pump or screw-like mechanism. The gardens were considered an engineering marvel because Babylon was located in a dry region.

How it was destroyed

Destruction

The Hanging Gardens were likely destroyed by earthquakes over time. After Babylon declined and was abandoned, the irrigation system stopped working, which would have caused the plants to die. No confirmed ruins have been found, leading some historians to debate whether the gardens actually existed or were exaggerated by ancient writers.
