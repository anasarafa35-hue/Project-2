7) Colossus of Rhodes (Greece)

The Colossus of Rhodes was a giant bronze statue. It honored the sun god Helios. It was built after a victory. It stood near the harbor.

Ships saw the statue as they arrived. It welcomed visitors. People felt pride. It symbolized freedom.

Bronze from weapons was reused. War materials became art. This showed strength. It took years to build.

An earthquake destroyed the statue. Even broken, it amazed people. Travelers visited the remains. Its image lived on.

Fun Facts

  • The statue stood for about 56 years.
  • Helios wore a crown of sun rays.
  • Ancient coins showed the statue.

How it was built

The Colossus of Rhodes was built around 280 BCE to honor the sun god Helios. It was a bronze statue supported by an iron framework and stood about 110 feet tall. Bronze plates were attached to the frame, and stone was used at the base for stability. The statue was built gradually using ramps.

How it was destroyed

An earthquake struck Rhodes in 226 BCE, causing the statue to break at the knees and collapse. The fallen pieces remained on the ground for hundreds of years. Eventually, the bronze was taken and melted down after an invasion.
