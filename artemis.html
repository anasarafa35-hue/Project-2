5) Temple of Artemis at Ephesus (Turkey)

The Temple of Artemis honored the goddess Artemis. It stood in Ephesus. It was one of the largest temples. Visitors were amazed.

The temple had marble columns and carvings. Artists worked for many years. Their skill was impressive. The design was detailed.

The temple was used for worship and trade. It became a social center. Religion and daily life mixed. Many people gathered.

The temple was destroyed many times. Only ruins remain today. Its history lives on. It is still remembered.

Fun Facts

  • It took over 100 years to build.
  • It stood on marshy ground.
  • It had over 100 columns.

How it was built

The Temple of Artemis was completed around 550 BCE and built entirely from marble. It featured 127 tall columns and was one of the largest temples in the ancient world. The temple was dedicated to the goddess Artemis and took many decades to complete. It was decorated with detailed sculptures and carvings.

How it was destroyed

The temple was burned down in 356 BCE by a man seeking fame. It was later rebuilt but suffered further damage from invasions and earthquakes. As the area declined and beliefs changed, the temple was abandoned and its stones were reused for other buildings.
