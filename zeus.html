4) Statue of Zeus at Olympia (Greece)

The Statue of Zeus stood inside a temple at Olympia. It honored Zeus, the king of the gods. Visitors traveled far to see it. The statue inspired awe.

The statue was made of gold and ivory. Zeus sat on a large throne. The statue filled the temple. People felt small before it.

Olympia hosted the Olympic Games. Athletes believed Zeus watched them. Religion and sports were connected. The statue made the games sacred.

The statue was destroyed by fire. Nothing remains today. Written records describe it. Its influence remains.

Fun Facts

  • The sculptor Phidias created it.
  • Zeus held a statue of Nike.
  • Oil protected it from moisture.

How It Was Built..

Construction

The Statue of Zeus was built around 435 BCE by the famous sculptor Phidias. It was constructed inside the Temple of Zeus at Olympia. The statue had a wooden framework that was covered with ivory to represent skin and gold to represent clothing and ornaments. Zeus was shown sitting on a large throne decorated with precious stones and carvings. The statue stood about 40 feet tall and nearly touched the ceiling of the temple. It symbolized power, authority, and religious devotion.

How it was destroyed

Destruction

The statue survived for several centuries but was eventually damaged or destroyed by fire. When the Roman Empire began banning pagan worship, the temple was closed. The statue may have been moved to Constantinople, where it was later destroyed in a fire, or it may have been destroyed at Olympia. No physical remains survive today.
