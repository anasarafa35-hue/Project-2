1) Great Pyramid of Giza (Egypt)

The Great Pyramid of Giza was built as a tomb for Pharaoh Khufu. It stands near the Nile River. The pyramid was meant to prepare the pharaoh for the afterlife. Religion was very important to Egyptians.

Millions of stone blocks were used to build the pyramid. Workers moved them without modern machines. Their math and planning skills were advanced. This shows their intelligence.

The pyramid was once covered in smooth white limestone. It reflected sunlight brightly. People could see it from far away. It symbolized power.

The Great Pyramid is the only wonder still standing today. It survived time and weather. Its design was very strong. No other ancient structure compares.

Fun Facts

  • It took about 20 years to build.
  • Smaller pyramids were built nearby for queens.
  • The entrance is hidden high above the ground.

How It Was Built..

Construction

The Great Pyramid was built around 2600 BCE as a tomb for Pharaoh Khufu. Ancient Egyptians used approximately 2.3 million limestone blocks, some weighing up to 15 tons. The stones were cut using copper tools and transported using sleds pulled across sand that was likely wet to reduce friction. Workers built ramps made of mudbrick and limestone to move the stones into place. The pyramid was carefully aligned with the cardinal directions, showing advanced knowledge of mathematics and astronomy. It took about 20 years to complete and was built by skilled laborers, not slaves.

How it was destroyed

Destruction

The Great Pyramid was never fully destroyed and is the only Ancient Wonder still standing today. Over time, earthquakes loosened the smooth outer casing stones. Many of these stones were later removed and reused in buildings in Cairo. Weathering and erosion have also worn down the structure, but its core remains intact.
